Conclusion-

I have found that former president Bill Clinton's statement, is for the most part true, but not to the extent originally
stated. His exact claims were that during the 52 years from 1961 to 2012, that despite the democratic party holding
office for only 24 years, as opposed to the republican parties' 28, they produced 42 million of the 66 million private
sector jobs that were produced.

I took both the data provided, and got my own copy from the government bureau of labor statistics, and ran the math.
I sorted the data into lists before subtracting the total jobs for the month at the start of a presidency
from the total jobs for the month at the end of a presidency. I did the math by month as to get a more accurate
result as opposed to going year by year. While finding the total gain or loss for each president, I also sorted
them by party and added up the running total per party.
My final results with the data sheet provided were:
Final Results : Democratic Party 36.92 Million
Final Results : Democratic Party 28.685 Million
Total : 65.605 Million

and with the data I downloaded directly from the government bureau it was:
Final Results : Democratic Party 37.512 Million
Final Results : Democratic Party 28.93 Million
Total : 66.442 Million

as you can see it varies slightly as the two were a few numbers off. Luckily the numbers are close enough as to not
really affect the end result too much. So, with this data, as I mentioned before, while his statement was accurate in
that more jobs were produced during the democratic presidencies, the results were not so drastic as he stated. Another
important factor to consider is that many policies results are often seen after a presidents term, causing the successor
to reap either the benefits or the consequences. So, I while wouldn't say what he was implying is necessarily true and 
his statement doesn't quite match the numbers, he was correct that more were "produced" during the democratic
presidencies.
